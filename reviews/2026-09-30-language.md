# Language read-through, 2026-09-30 (non-native, not a native-speaker check)

This is a machine read-through of the Afrikaans and isiZulu spreads in `logs/2026-09-30.md` (Episode 2 light refine and the new Episode 3 draft). It flags phrases that may read as unnatural or translated, so a real native speaker has a shorter list to check later. **This is not a native-speaker check and does not clear either language for publishing.**

## Afrikaans

1. "Baylin het gejaag" (Ep 2, Bladsy-oordrag 3, for "Baylin rushed") — same issue flagged on 2026-09-29: "gejaag" more commonly means chased or driven fast; "gehaas" may be closer to the intended meaning. Carried into this refine unchanged.
2. "die middel-leidraad gefluister" (Ep 2, Bladsy-oordrag 5) and "Die middel-leidraad het opgestyg" (Ep 3, Bladsy-oordrag 7) — "middel-leidraad" is a literal rendering of the English production term "mid cue" and still reads as scripting jargon leaking into the story text, not natural prose. Same issue flagged on 2026-09-29, now appearing in both episodes.
3. "Baylin het geknipoog" (Ep 3, Bladsy-oordrag 2, for "Baylin squinted") — "geknipoog" ordinarily means "winked," not "squinted." This looks like a mistranslation rather than a style choice; worth a native check before the two are confused.
4. "Sy het om meer lig, meer geraas, meer vonkel gevra" (Ep 3, Bladsy-oordrag 4) — splitting "om ... gevra" this far apart reads awkwardly; a more natural order is likely "Sy het gevra om meer lig, meer geraas, meer vonkel."
5. "net 'n kamee" (Ep 3, Bladsy-oordrag 10, for "just a cameo") — borrows the English/film term "kamee" directly; may read as jargon rather than storybook language. Worth checking for a more natural Afrikaans phrase (for example "net 'n kort besoekie").

## isiZulu

Confidence here is lower than for Afrikaans; treat all of these as "ask a native speaker," not corrections.

1. "kuhlebela iNyanga Eleleyo" (Ep 2, Isakazelo 2) — the subject concord "ku-" reads as an impersonal/expletive form, while "iNyanga" (class 9) would more typically take a "ya-" concord (for example "yahlebela"). Worth a native check on subject-verb agreement here.
2. "Isakazelo" used throughout as the word for "Spread" (picture-book page-turn) — this looks like a coined term rather than an established isiZulu word for the concept; a native speaker may prefer a different word or a loanword handled differently.
3. "kameo nje" (Ep 3, Isakazelo 10, for "just a cameo") — same loanword concern as the Afrikaans "kamee" above; borrows the English film term directly rather than using a native isiZulu phrase.
4. "Isiqondiso esiphakathi senyuka" (Ep 3, Isakazelo 7, for "the mid cue floated up") — same issue as the Afrikaans "middel-leidraad": a direct translation of the English stage-direction "mid cue," not natural narrative text, and a strong candidate to cut or rephrase before a native reviewer sees it.
