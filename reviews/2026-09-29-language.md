# Language read-through, 2026-09-29 (non-native, not a native-speaker check)

This is a machine read-through of the Afrikaans and isiZulu spreads in `logs/2026-09-29.md` (Episode 1 refine and Episode 2 draft). It flags phrases that may read as unnatural or translated, so a real native speaker has a shorter list to check later. **This is not a native-speaker check and does not clear either language for publishing.**

## Afrikaans

1. "gezoem" / "het gezoem" (Ep 1, Spread 7 and Ep 2 nowhere) — the "ge-" prefix looks doubled up on a stem that may not need it this way; flag for spelling ("gesoem" may be the intended form).
2. "die *gezoem van die wind*" (Ep 1, Spread 7) — same spelling question as above, repeated.
3. "so hard gesing as wat sy kon" (Ep 1, Spread 3) — word order reads like a direct English translation of "sang as loudly as she could"; a native speaker may prefer a more idiomatic Afrikaans phrasing.
4. "Ons onthou die *getik*" (Ep 1, Spread 6, grasshoppers) — "getik" (ticking) as the sound word for grasshoppers may not be the most natural Afrikaans onomatopoeia choice; worth checking against a more idiomatic word.
5. "Dit is net uitgedeel" (Ep 1, Spread 11, about the song) — "uitgedeel" (handed out) is an unusual collocation for a song being shared; may read oddly to a native ear.
6. "Die middel-leidraad het soos 'n klein wolkie opgestyg" (Ep 2, Spread 5) — "middel-leidraad" is a literal rendering of the English production term "mid cue" and reads as scripting jargon leaking into the story text rather than natural prose; likely worth cutting or rewriting for the storybook version.
7. "Baylin het gejaag" (Ep 2, Spread 3, for "Baylin rushed") — "gejaag" more commonly means chased or driven fast; "gehaas" may be closer to the intended meaning.
8. "Sy het 'n kombers na die lug gewaai" (Ep 2, Spread 3) — "na die lug gewaai" (waved a blanket "to" the sky) reads slightly off; "in die lug gewaai" may be more natural.

## isiZulu

Confidence here is lower than for Afrikaans; treat all of these as "ask a native speaker," not corrections.

1. "Ngiyikhohliwe ingoma yami yemvula" (Ep 1, Spread 2) — two possessive/qualifier elements stacked back to back ("yami yemvula") may or may not be natural word order; worth a native check.
2. Onomatopoeia kept in the same form across all three languages ("*Dum-dum. Dum-dum.*", "*Thi-thi-thi.*" in isiZulu vs "*Tip-tip-tip.*" in English) — sound words are usually language-specific; using near-identical syllables in English, Afrikaans and isiZulu risks sounding foreign in at least one of the three. Worth asking a native speaker for authentic isiZulu (and Afrikaans) equivalents rather than reusing the English pattern.
3. Mixed localization of names — "Sunhill" is translated as "iLanga"/"Gqumeni leLanga," but "Tilly" and "Rainbird" are only partly localized ("iNyoni yeMvula" for Rainbird, but "Tilly" itself stays untranslated). Worth confirming with Kevin and a native speaker whether this mix is intentional or should be made consistent.
4. "Isiqondiso esiphakathi senyuka njengefu elincane" (Ep 2, Spread 5) — same issue as the Afrikaans "middel-leidraad" above: this looks like a direct translation of the English stage-direction "mid cue," not natural narrative text, and is a strong candidate to cut before this goes to a native reviewer.
