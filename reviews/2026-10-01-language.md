# Language read-through, 2026-10-01 (non-native, not a native-speaker check)

This is a machine read-through of the Afrikaans and isiZulu spreads in `logs/2026-10-01.md` (new Episode 4 draft) and `handoff/youtube/2026-10-01.md`. It flags phrases that may read as unnatural, invented, or translated, so a real native speaker has a shorter list to check later. **This is not a native-speaker check and does not clear either language for publishing.**

## Afrikaans

1. "Ek jaag jou na die wilgerboom!" (Spread 1, for "Race you to the willow tree!") — "jaag" more commonly means to hurry, chase, or drive fast, not "to race someone" in the competitive-game sense. A phrase like "Ek baklei jou na die wilgerboom!" or "Kom ons hardloop wedloop na die wilgerboom!" may read more naturally. Same root concern as "gejaag"/"gehaas" flagged on 2026-09-29 and 2026-09-30.
2. "Die middel-leidraad het oor die water opgestyg" (Spread 7, for "the mid cue floated up") — same recurring issue flagged on 2026-09-29 and 2026-09-30: "middel-leidraad" is a literal rendering of the English production term "mid cue" and reads as scripting jargon leaking into story prose, now appearing in a fourth episode. Still worth one consistent decision from Kevin or a native speaker rather than a fifth flag next episode.
3. "'n Jammer wat aanhou hardloop, is nog nie klaar nie" (Spread 5, for "A sorry that keeps running isn't finished yet") — treating "jammer" (an interjection/adjective meaning "sorry") as a noun that can "run" is a direct carry-over of the English metaphor; may not land the same way in Afrikaans. A native speaker may prefer rephrasing around the action rather than personifying the apology.
4. "Nou stamp dit net..." (Spread 6, for "Now it just... bumps") — short and plain; worth checking it reads as wistful rather than just abrupt in context.

## isiZulu

Confidence here is lower than for Afrikaans; treat all of these as "ask a native speaker," not corrections.

1. "kumuthi wesilibha" (Isakazelo 1, for "to the willow tree") — "isilibha" as a word for "willow" is uncertain; this may be a coined or imprecise term rather than an established isiZulu name for the tree. Worth checking whether a native speaker uses a loanword or a different term entirely.
2. "Ngikugijimisa size sifike kumuthi wesilibha!" (Isakazelo 1, for "Race you to the willow tree!") — the causative-looking "ngikugijimisa" ("I make you run") may not carry the playful "race you" sense intended; subject-object agreement and the intended meaning both need a native check.
3. "Izinyawo zikaBaylin zaphaxaza emhlangeni" (Isakazelo 2, for "Baylin's feet splashed through the reeds") — "phaxaza" as a splashing verb is uncertain vocabulary; flagging for a native speaker to confirm or replace.
4. "Isiqondiso esiphakathi senyuka phezu kwamanzi" (Isakazelo 7, for "the mid cue floated up over the water") — same recurring issue as the Afrikaans "middel-leidraad": a direct translation of the English stage-direction "mid cue," not natural narrative text, now in a fourth episode.
5. "Isakazelo" used again throughout for "Spread" — same coined-term concern raised on 2026-09-30; carried forward unchanged pending one decision on terminology.
